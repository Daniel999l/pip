the little bird

there was a small bird in a big green tree. the bird was brown and the bird was small. her name was pip. pip lived in a nest at the top of the tree. the nest was soft and warm.

every morning the sun came up. the sun was red and then it was gold. pip woke up and looked at the sky. the sky was big and it was blue. pip said, i want to sing.

pip's mother could sing. her father could sing too. they sang in the morning and they sang at night. their songs were soft and sweet. pip liked to hear the songs. she sat in the nest and she listened.

one day pip opened her beak. she tried to sing. but it was not a song. it was a small squeak. squeak, squeak, squeak. pip was sad. she said, i can't sing.

her mother said, you can sing, pip. you just have to learn. a song is not made in one day. it is made a little at a time. you try and you try. then one day it is there.

pip said, how do i learn? her mother said, you listen to the birds, and you try again. every day you try a little more. pip said, i will try.

the next morning pip sat on the branch. the wind was soft. the leaves were green. pip listened to the birds in the trees. one bird sang a high note. one bird sang a low note. pip tried the high note. squeak! pip tried the low note. squeak!

pip was not happy, but she was not done. she said, i will try again tomorrow. and she did.

the next day pip tried again. this time her squeak was a little bit longer. it was not a song, but it was better. her mother smiled. the next day it was better still.

every day pip sang a little more. she sang to the sun. she sang to the wind. she sang to the leaves on the tree. she sang to the clouds in the sky. some days it was good and some days it was not. but she did not stop.

one evening the sky turned pink. the air was cool and still. pip sat on the branch and she opened her beak. and out came a song. it was a small song, but it was a real song. it went up and it went down. it was sweet and clear.

the birds in the trees were quiet. they all listened to pip. her mother was so happy. her father was so happy too. pip was the happiest of all.

she said, i did it! i can sing! her mother said, yes, you can. you tried and you tried, and now you can sing. pip sang again and again, until the stars came out.

that night pip slept in the nest, in the soft warm hay. she had a song in her heart. and in the morning she woke up and sang it to the sun.

the cat and the rain

there was a cat named milo. milo was a grey cat with white feet. he lived in a little house with a red door. he liked to sleep in the sun and he liked to eat fish.

one day it was grey outside. the clouds were low and dark. milo sat at the window and looked out. he said, what is that? a drop of rain fell on the glass. then another drop fell. then many drops fell.

milo had not seen rain before. he was a young cat. he looked at the drops and the drops ran down the window. he put his paw on the glass. the drops ran away from his paw.

milo said, i want to go out. the door was shut. but the girl in the house opened it. she said, go on, milo, but come back soon. milo walked out into the garden.

the rain was cold. it fell on his head and it fell on his back. milo said, oh no! he ran back to the door. his fur was wet and his feet were wet. the girl laughed. she took a soft towel and she dried him.

milo sat by the fire. he was warm and dry. he looked out at the rain. the rain was still falling. it fell on the roof, tap, tap, tap. it fell on the grass, and the grass was happy.

milo said, i like the rain, but i like it best from here. he closed his eyes. the fire was warm and the rain was soft. soon he was asleep, and he dreamed of fish.

in the morning the rain was gone. the sun came out and the garden was shiny. there were drops on every leaf. there was a bright bow in the sky, red and orange and yellow and green and blue. milo sat in the door and looked at it.

the girl said, do you like it? milo said, mew. she knew what that meant. it meant yes.

the soup

mama made soup on a cold day. the soup was hot and it smelled so good. there were carrots in the soup and there were beans in the soup. there was a little salt, and there was a lot of love.

tom sat at the table. his sister sat at the table too. the bowls were warm. the spoons were shiny. tom said, i want soup! his sister said, so do i!

mama put the soup in the bowls. she put a piece of bread by each bowl. the bread was soft in the middle and hard on the outside. tom took a bite of the bread. he took a spoon of the soup. it was so good.

his sister said, is there more? mama said, yes, there is more. there is a big pot of soup. eat as much as you like. the soup was hot, so they blew on it. blow, blow, blow. then they ate it up.

outside the wind was cold. the trees were bare. but inside the kitchen it was warm and bright. the window was white with steam. tom drew a face on the window with his finger. his sister drew a house.

papa came home and he was cold. his nose was red. mama gave him a bowl of soup. he said, this is the best soup in the world. mama smiled. she said, you say that every time. papa said, it is true every time.

after the soup they all sat by the fire. mama read a story. tom listened and his sister listened. the fire went crackle, crackle, crackle. one by one their eyes closed. it was a good day and a good night.

the town in the morning

the town woke up slowly. first the sky got light. then the birds began to sing. then a door opened and a man came out with a broom. he swept the step in front of his shop. sweep, sweep, sweep.

the baker was up before all the others. his bakery was warm and it smelled of bread. he put the loaves on the shelf. he put the buns in the window. there were big loaves and small loaves, and some had seeds on top.

a girl came by with a basket. she said, good morning! the baker said, good morning! what would you like? she said, one loaf, please. the baker put a loaf in her basket. it was still warm. she said, thank you, and she went on her way.

the milk man came down the road with his cart. the horse walked slow and steady. clip, clop, clip, clop. the milk man left a bottle at every door. the bottles were cold and white.

the clock in the square struck eight. the school bell rang. children ran down the street with bags on their backs. some were fast and some were slow. a small boy stopped to look at a dog. the dog wagged its tail. the boy said, i have to go to school, but i will see you after.

the shops opened one by one. the man at the fruit shop put out his apples and his pears. the woman at the flower shop put out roses and daisies. the air smelled of bread and flowers and fresh rain.

by noon the town was full. people talked and people laughed. a band played in the square, and a small dog danced. the sun was high and it was warm. it was a good day to be in the town.

the sea

the sea is big and the sea is blue. it goes on and on, as far as you can see. the waves come in and the waves go out. they come in with a splash, and they go out with a sigh.

a boy named sam lived by the sea. his house was small and white. it had a blue door and a round window. from the window he could see the water. every morning he looked at the sea and said hello.

sam had a small boat. the boat was red and it was old. it had one sail and two oars. his grandfather gave it to him. his grandfather said, the sea is kind if you are kind to it. sam did not forget.

one morning the sea was calm. there was no wind and no waves. the water was like glass. sam put the boat in the water and he rowed out. dip, pull, dip, pull. the boat went slow and smooth.

he saw a fish jump. he saw a bird dive. he saw a crab on a rock. the crab looked at sam and sam looked at the crab. the crab went into a hole. sam laughed.

then the wind came. it was a small wind, then a big wind. the sail went tight and the boat went fast. sam held on. he was not afraid, because he knew the sea. he turned the boat and he went home.

on the sand his mother was waiting. she said, did you have a good time? sam said, yes! i saw a fish and a bird and a crab. his mother said, you must be hungry. sam said, yes, i am very hungry.

they ate fish and bread by the sea. the sun went down and the sky was orange. the waves came in and the waves went out. sam sat on the sand and he watched them. he thought, i love the sea.

the little fish

a little fish lived in a big reef. the reef was full of color. there were red fish and yellow fish and blue fish. there were plants that moved in the water. there were shells on the sand.

the little fish was silver. she was the smallest fish in the reef. the big fish swam fast and she swam slow. she said, i wish i was big.

an old turtle swam by. the turtle was slow and wise. he said, why are you sad, little fish? she said, i am too small. the turtle said, small is not bad. you can go where big fish can't go.

the little fish thought about it. she swam into a small hole in the rock. it was dark in the hole, but there was a light at the end. she swam on and she came out in a cave. the cave was full of pearls. they shone like little moons.

she swam back to tell the others. the big fish could not fit in the hole. so the little fish took them each a pearl. they were so happy. they said, thank you, little fish! you are the best.

the little fish smiled. she was still small, but now she did not mind. she knew that small was a good thing to be. and every night she swam home to her hole in the rock, and slept.

the garden

in the garden there was a small seed. it was brown and it was hard. a girl put it in the dirt. she said, grow, little seed. then she gave it some water and she went in.

the seed was in the dark. it was warm and wet in the dirt. for a long time nothing happened. the girl came every day and she looked. there was nothing. she said, maybe it can't grow. but she gave it water.

one morning there was a small green thing. it was a little leaf. then there were two leaves. the girl was so happy. she ran to tell her father. he came and he looked. he said, look at that! it is growing.

the plant grew and it grew. the sun made it warm and the rain made it strong. the stem got long and the leaves got big. soon it was as tall as the girl. then it had a bud.

the bud was green and tight. one day it opened. it was a flower, a big yellow flower. it looked like the sun. bees came to visit. a bird sat on a leaf. the girl smiled all day.

the flower gave seeds. the girl picked them. she put them in a bag. next spring she would plant them all. there would be a lot of flowers. the garden would be yellow and bright.

the four seasons

in spring the world wakes up. the snow melts and the grass is green. the buds come out on the trees. the birds come back and they build their nests. the air is soft and it smells of rain. the lambs jump in the field.

in summer the days are long. the sun is hot and the sky is blue. we swim in the sea and we eat cold fruit. the bees are busy in the flowers. we stay up late and watch the stars come out. the nights are warm.

in autumn the leaves turn red and gold. they fall from the trees and they make a soft carpet. the wind is cool. the apples are ripe and we pick them. the geese fly south in a long line. it gets dark early.

in winter it is cold. the snow falls and the ground is white. the lake is ice. we wear hats and scarves and warm boots. we make a snow man with a carrot nose. at night we sit by the fire and drink hot milk.

then the year goes round. the snow melts again and the grass is green. spring comes back, and the world wakes up. it is the same and it is new. that is the way of the year.

the night sky

when the sun goes down the sky gets dark. first one star comes out, then two, then many. soon the whole sky is full of little lights. they shine and shine, far away.

the moon comes up. some nights the moon is round and bright. some nights it is a thin curve, like a smile. it moves across the sky and it lights up the dark. the cat sits on the wall and looks at it. the owl calls from the tree. who, who.

a boy lay on the grass and looked up. he said, how many stars are there? his father said, more than we can count. the boy said, are they near? his father said, no, they are very far. the light you see left them a long, long time ago.

the boy thought about this. he said, so the stars are old. his father said, yes, they are old. but they are still shining. the boy was quiet for a while. then he said, i want to see them all.

they lay on the grass until it was late. a star fell, a quick bright line. the boy said, i made a wish. his father said, i hope it comes true. then they went in and the boy went to bed.

he looked out the window as he fell asleep. the stars were still there. they will be there tomorrow night and the night after that. they are always there, even when we can't see them.

the school day

the bell rang and the children came in. they put their bags on the hooks. they put their coats on the hooks too. the teacher said, good morning, class! the class said, good morning!

the room was bright. there were pictures on the wall. there was a big map and a clock. the desks were in rows and each desk had a chair. a plant sat on the window.

first they did reading. the teacher held up a book. it had a picture of a duck. she said, what is this? the class said, a duck! she said, yes. what does a duck say? the class said, quack, quack! everyone laughed.

then they did numbers. the teacher put counters on the desk. a girl put them in a row. she counted them out loud. she did it right, and the teacher said, well done.

at noon it was time to eat. the children sat at the long table. they had bread and cheese and an apple. a boy had a pear. a girl had a sweet cake. they shared and they talked.

after lunch they went out to play. some played ball and some played tag. some sat under the tree and told stories. the sun was warm and the wind was soft. the bell rang and they went back in.

in the afternoon they drew. the teacher said, draw your home. the children took their pens and made pictures. one drew a house with a tall roof. one drew a boat. one drew a tree with a swing. the teacher put them all on the wall.

at the end of the day the teacher said, you did well today. see you tomorrow! the children said, goodbye! they put on their coats and they went home. the school was quiet. the plant sat on the window in the last of the sun.

the dog and the hill

a dog named bo lived on a farm. bo was big and brown and he had one floppy ear. he liked to run and he liked to dig. he liked to sleep by the barn in the sun.

behind the farm there was a hill. it was green and it was steep. bo looked at the hill every day. he said to himself, what is at the top? he did not know. he had never been there.

one morning bo got up early. the grass was wet. the air was cool. he walked to the hill and he began to climb. it was hard. his legs were tired and his paws were sore. but he went on and on.

halfway up he stopped. he looked back. the farm was small now. the barn was a little red box. the cows were little dots. bo said, it is a long way down. but he did not go down. he climbed some more.

at last he got to the top. the wind blew in his ears. he could see the whole world, the fields and the river and the road. there was a town far away. there was a lake that shone in the sun.

bo sat down and he looked. he was so happy. he had not known the world was so big. he stayed there for a long time. then the sun began to go down, and he went home.

the farmer said, where have you been, bo? bo wagged his tail. he could not say. but he had a look in his eyes that said, i saw the world.

the baker's bread

in the town there was a baker. every night he made the dough. he put in flour and water and a little salt. he put in a bit of yeast. he mixed it and he pressed it with his hands.

then he let it sit. the dough was quiet and warm. slowly it got bigger and bigger. it was like magic. the baker said, the dough is alive. it wants time.

in the dark of the night he lit the oven. the fire was hot and bright. he put the loaves in and shut the door. the shop began to smell so good. the smell went out the window and down the street.

a cat woke up and sniffed. a man woke up and sniffed. a baby woke up and she sniffed. everybody in the town knew that the bread was ready.

the baker took the loaves out. they were golden and round. he tapped one and it sounded hollow. that is how he knew it was done. he put them on the shelf to cool.

in the morning the people came. they bought the bread and they took it home. they ate it with butter and with jam. they ate it with soup and with cheese. and the baker went to bed, and slept all day.

the wind

the wind is a thing you can't see. but you can see what it does. it moves the leaves and it bends the grass. it pushes the clouds across the sky. it makes the kite fly high.

a girl had a red kite. she took it to the top of the field. her brother held the string. he ran and ran, and the kite went up. at first it fell down. then the wind came, and it lifted the kite.

the kite went higher and higher. it danced in the sky. it was red against the blue. the girl held the string tight. she said, look at it go! her brother said, it is going to the clouds.

the wind got strong. the string pulled hard. the girl held on with both hands. but the wind was stronger, and the string slipped. the kite flew away. it went over the trees and over the hill. it got smaller and smaller, and then it was gone.

the girl was sad. her brother said, we can make another one. the girl said, i know. but i liked that one. she looked at the sky. she said, maybe someone will find it. maybe the kite will make a child happy far away.

that night they made a new kite. it was yellow. they put a long tail on it. in the morning they went to the field, and the wind was kind. the yellow kite flew up and up.

the bear and the honey

a bear lived in a cave at the edge of a wood. he was big and he was slow. he liked to sleep and he liked to eat. most of all he liked honey.

one day the bear woke up and he was hungry. his cupboard was empty. he said, i need honey. he put on his hat and he walked into the wood.

he found a tall tree. high in the tree there was a hive. he could hear the bees. buzz, buzz, buzz. the bear began to climb. the branch bent under him. he went up slow and careful.

the bees did not like it. they came out in a cloud. they went round and round his head. the bear said, i only want a little honey. but the bees did not hear. one bee sat on his nose.

the bear slid down the tree. bump, bump, bump. he ran to the river and he jumped in. the water was cool. the bees went home. the bear sat in the river and he was sad.

then he saw a girl on the bank. she had a jar. she said, i have honey. do you want some? the bear said, yes, please. she gave him the jar. the bear ate it all up. it was sweet and it was good.

the bear said, thank you. the girl said, you are welcome. from that day the bear and the girl were friends. every week she brought him a jar. and every week the bear brought her a flower from the wood.

two friends

there was a duck and there was a frog. they lived by the same pond. the duck was white and the frog was green. the duck could fly and the frog could jump.

every day they played together. they swam in the pond and they sat on the log. they watched the clouds go by. they told each other stories. the duck told of the sky and the frog told of the mud.

one day the duck said, i have to go away. it is winter and i must fly south. the frog was sad. he said, will you come back? the duck said, yes. i will come back in spring.

the duck flew away. the frog sat on the log. the days got short and cold. the pond froze over. the frog went down into the mud and he slept. he dreamed of the duck.

at last the ice melted. the sun was warm. the frog woke up and he climbed on the log. he looked at the sky. he waited and waited. and then he saw it, a white shape in the blue.

it was the duck! she landed on the pond with a splash. the frog jumped for joy. the duck said, i told you i would come back. the frog said, i knew you would. they sat on the log, and they told each other all their stories.

the lighthouse

on a rock by the sea there was a tall white lighthouse. at the top there was a big light. every night the keeper climbed the stairs and he lit the lamp. the light went round and round. it shone over the water.

the ships saw the light from far away. they knew where the rocks were. they knew where the shore was. the light kept them safe. the keeper was proud of his work.

one night a storm came. the wind howled and the rain beat on the glass. the waves were as high as houses. the keeper stood by the lamp. he said, the ships need me tonight.

he kept the light bright. he cleaned the glass. he watched the sea. far out he saw a small boat. it was tossed up and down. the light shone on it. the people on the boat saw the shore. they turned and they came in.

in the morning the storm was gone. the sea was calm and the sky was clear. the people came to the lighthouse. they said, thank you. you saved us. the keeper said, it is only a light. but he was glad.

that night the keeper climbed the stairs again. he lit the lamp, and the light went round and round.

the cold morning

it was a cold morning. the window was white with frost. the grass was stiff. the pond had a skin of ice. a small boy put on his hat and his coat. he put on his boots and his red scarf. he said, i am going out.

he opened the door and the cold air hit his face. his breath made a cloud. he laughed and made more clouds. puff, puff, puff. he walked down the path and the frost crunched under his boots.

in the garden a robin sat on the fence. the robin was round and it had a red chest. the boy said, hello! the robin looked at him. the boy had some crumbs in his pocket. he put them on the post. the robin hopped down and ate.

the boy found a long icicle on the roof. it was clear like glass. he broke it off and held it up. the sun shone through it and made a small rainbow. he put it in his mouth. it was so cold! he made a face and threw it away.

then he heard his mother call. she said, breakfast! the boy ran in. the kitchen was warm and it smelled of toast. he took off his boots. he took off his scarf. he sat at the table and ate his egg.

the toy shop

at the end of the street there was a toy shop. the window was full of toys. there was a wooden horse and a tin drum. there was a doll with a blue dress. there was a train that went round and round on a track.

the old man who owned the shop made the toys himself. he sat at a bench in the back. he had a small saw and a small hammer. he had paint in little pots. every day he made something new.

children stopped at the window. they put their noses on the glass. they pointed at the train. they pointed at the horse. the old man waved from the back. he liked to see them smile.

one day a small girl came into the shop. she had a coin in her hand. she said, how much is the bear? the old man looked at the bear. it was brown and soft, with button eyes. he said, it costs one coin. the girl gave him the coin. she held the bear tight.

she said, thank you. the old man said, take good care of him. the girl said, i will. she went out into the street with the bear in her arms. the old man went back to his bench. he took a piece of wood and he began to make another bear.

the rainy day

it rained all day. the sky was grey and the street was wet. a girl and her brother sat inside and looked at the window. they said, there is nothing to do.

their mother said, you can make a fort. so they took the pillows from the sofa. they took the blankets from the beds. they put a chair here and a chair there. they put a sheet on top. soon they had a fort.

inside the fort it was dim and cozy. they took a lamp and some books. they took some cookies and a jug of milk. the rain tapped on the roof of the house. the fort was a ship on the sea. the girl was the captain and her brother was the crew.

the captain said, we are sailing to a far land. the crew said, i see a storm! they rocked from side to side. the pillows were waves. they sailed and sailed. at last they came to a land full of cake. they ate cookies and they were happy.

when the rain stopped, the sun came out. the girl looked out of the fort. the street was shiny. she said, it is time to go out. but the brother said, not yet. we are still on the ship. so they stayed in the fort a little longer.

the owl

in the old oak tree lived an owl. she had big round eyes and soft grey wings. all day she slept in a hole in the tree. at night she woke up and she looked at the dark.

the owl could see in the dark. she could hear the smallest sound. a mouse in the grass, a beetle on a leaf. she sat on a branch and she listened. she was still, and she was patient.

a young rabbit came out of his hole. he was afraid of the owl. but the owl was not hunting that night. she was tired and she was full. she said, hello, little rabbit. why are you out so late?

the rabbit said, i could not sleep. the owl said, the night is a good time to think. look at the stars. listen to the wind. the rabbit sat down. he looked up. the night was quiet and wide.

they sat there until the sky began to get light. the owl said, i must go to bed now. the rabbit said, thank you for the night. the owl flew to her hole. the rabbit hopped home. the sun came up over the hill.

the market

on saturday there was a market in the square. the stalls were under bright cloth. there were apples and pears and plums. there were cheese and eggs and honey. there were hats and bags and flowers.

a woman sold fish. she called out, fresh fish! fresh fish! a man sold bread. he called out, hot bread! hot bread! a boy sold flowers. he called out, a rose for you! a rose for you!

mother and daughter walked through the market. the girl held her mother's hand. she saw a stall with red apples. she said, can we have some? her mother said, yes. we will take six. the man put them in a bag.

they bought bread and they bought cheese. they bought a bunch of yellow flowers. the girl carried the flowers. she smelled them. they smelled like the sun. she said, i love the market. her mother said, so do i.

at noon the stalls began to close. the people packed up their things. the square got quiet. a dog walked through and found a crust of bread. the pigeons came down and walked about. the market was over for another week.

the moon and the river

a river ran through the wood. it was clear and cold and it ran fast over the stones. in the day the river sang. in the night the river was still and the moon looked down into it.

the moon said, river, where do you go? the river said, i go to the sea. the moon said, what is the sea like? the river said, i have never seen it. i only know that i must go. so i run, and i run.

the moon thought about this. she said, i go round and round the earth. i have never been to the sea either. but i see it every night. it is silver, like me. the river said, then i will like it.

each night the moon and the river talked. the moon told the river about the sea. the river told the moon about the wood. the fish listened. the deer listened. the reeds leaned in to hear.

at last the river came to the sea. it ran into the wide water. it was no longer a river. it was part of the sea. and that night the moon looked down, and she saw the river there. she smiled, and the water shone.

the old tree

at the edge of the town there was an old tree. it was so old that no one knew how old it was. its trunk was wide and its roots were deep. its branches reached out like arms.

children climbed the tree. they sat on its branches. they ate their lunch in its shade. birds built nests in it. squirrels ran up and down its bark. in autumn it dropped its leaves, and the children jumped in the pile.

the tree watched the town grow. it saw the first houses. it saw the road and the bridge. it saw the children grow up and have children of their own. it did not speak, but it remembered.

one summer there was a big storm. the wind blew hard and the rain came down. in the morning the people went to see the tree. a big branch had fallen. but the tree was still standing. its roots held fast.

the people cut the branch into planks. they made a bench. they put the bench under the tree. now everyone can sit in the shade and rest. the old tree is still there, and it still gives.

the snow day

in the night the snow came down. it fell soft and slow. it covered the roofs and the trees and the road. when the town woke up, everything was white.

the school was shut. the children cheered. they ran out in their coats and their hats. they threw snow at each other. they made snow angels. they slid down the hill on trays and on old boards.

a girl made a snow man. she rolled a big ball for the body and a small ball for the head. she put two stones for the eyes. she put a stick for the arms. her father gave her a carrot for the nose and a scarf for the neck.

the snow man stood in the garden. he looked at the house. he looked at the sky. the girl said, he is happy. her father said, he is, but he will be gone when the sun comes. the girl said, i know. but he is here today.

in the afternoon the sun came out. the snow shone so bright that it hurt the eyes. the snow man began to drip. drip, drip, drip. by night he was small. by morning he was only a scarf and two stones and a carrot on the grass.

the girl put the carrot in the soup. she kept the stones in a jar. she said, next winter we will make him again. and she did.

the first day of spring

the sun came up early. the air was soft. the snow was gone and the ground was brown and wet. a small green point came out of the dirt. then another. then many.

the farmer walked in his field. he said, spring is here. he took a handful of seed. he threw it on the ground. the birds came down and they ate some, but there was plenty left.

the cows went out to the grass. they walked slow and they ate and ate. the lambs ran and jumped. the hens scratched in the yard. the dog chased a butterfly and did not catch it.

in the town the windows were open. the people swept out the dust. they hung the wash on the line. the sheets went up and down in the breeze. a woman sang as she worked. a man whistled in his shop.

a small girl found a flower in the grass. it was white with a yellow heart. she picked it and ran to her mother. she said, look! the first flower! her mother put it in a cup of water on the table. all day it made the room feel like spring.

what the bird saw

a bird flew high over the land. she saw the green fields like squares of cloth. she saw the river like a silver thread. she saw the town with its red roofs and its white church. she saw the road that went up the hill.

she flew over the sea. it was wide and it was blue. she saw a ship, so small it was like a leaf. she saw a whale rise and blow. she saw a flock of gulls over the waves.

she flew over a wood. she saw deer under the trees. she saw a fox in the grass. she saw a stream and a pond and a little house with smoke from the chimney.

the sun was going down. the sky was gold. the bird was tired. she turned and she flew back. below her the lights of the town came on, one by one, yellow and warm.

she came down to her tree. she tucked her head under her wing. she said to herself, the world is big and the world is good. then she slept.

a song for the day

good morning, sun. good morning, sky. good morning, birds that fly so high. good morning, grass and good morning, tree. good morning, all that i can see.

good afternoon, bee. good afternoon, rain. good afternoon, wind on the window pane. good afternoon, bread and good afternoon, tea. good afternoon, all that is good to me.

good night, moon. good night, star. good night, all the lands so far. good night, house and good night, bed. good night, and rest your sleepy head.

the little lamp

in a small room there was a small lamp. it sat on a table by the bed. when the night was dark, the lamp gave light. it was warm and yellow. it made a round bright spot on the wall.

a boy slept in the bed. sometimes he was afraid of the dark. he said, mama, i don't like the dark. his mother said, you have your lamp. the lamp is here. it will stay with you all night. the boy looked at the lamp and he felt better.

the lamp could not talk. but it knew the boy. it knew his dreams. it knew when he turned over in his sleep. it knew when he woke up and looked around. it shone all night, a soft light in the dark room.

in the morning the sun came in at the window. the mother turned off the lamp. the lamp rested. it was proud of its work. it knew that it would shine again when the night came.

and the boy got up, and the day began, and the lamp waited for the dark.
